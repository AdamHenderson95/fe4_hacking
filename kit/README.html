fe4kit: FE4 hacking toolkit (Project Naga ROM)

Make hacks and randomized games of Fire Emblem: Genealogy of the Holy War (the Project Naga translation). You need your own copy of the ROM; the tools read everything out of it and never ship any of the game.

Builder of the Holy War, the editor (Windows)

Run BuilderOfTheHolyWar.exe. Nothing to install. It opens Builder of the Holy War in its own window (it uses the Edge browser that comes with Windows, in app mode; if Edge isn't there it uses Chrome, or opens in your default browser). The editor runs on your PC only: nothing is sent anywhere. (Up to kit v15 the editor was called FE4 Studio and the program FE4KitEditor.exe.)

  1. Project → Choose your ROM…, then New project… picks a folder and a name; the editor reads the whole game into that project folder. Open an existing project with Open a project folder… or from Recent.
  2. Characters shows every unit as a portrait (read from your ROM). Pick one to open their sheet, laid out like the game's status screen:
  3. name, class, level, gender, leadership stars and gold;
  4. Fighting skill: each stat's total (the class base plus the unit's own value) with a bar, the unit's own value and its growth;
  5. Holy blood: minor or major for each of the 13 bloodlines;
  6. Items (it warns when two units are given the same item), Skills (click to give or take away), Love partners and the game IDs.
  7. Children have their own sheet: the parent, who passes on the holy weapon, how much of the parents' growths and stats they inherit, and the skills they can inherit.
  8. Love & children: a check at the top says whether children, love and inheritance build (after every change).
  9. Love: one table per generation, women down and men across. Each square shows the love a couple gains per turn near each other and where they start (500 makes them lovers); × means never. Click a square to change it, or a name to set a whole row or column.
  10. Families: each parent with the children tied to them (born to that parent and whoever they marry). Change a child's parent here; click a child to open them on the Characters page for inheritance, stand-in and skills. + New child adds a brand-new child: name (up to 7 tiles wide), class, level, parent, the chapter they join (6–11), which parent's holy blood they get, a stand-in and a portrait to borrow (a name like "Lex" or a portrait number). The name takes one of the 6 free name slots, and the child joins at the start of that chapter. New children show a new tag and can be deleted (the newest first). See "New children" below for their limits (one extra child fits the save).
  11. Inheritance rules: children's gold and the highest stat they can inherit.
  12. Classes: bases, growths, weapon ranks, movement, flying and class skills, with the units that use each class.
  13. Skills: FE4's own skills and new ones, with a line at the top that says whether the skills build (checked after every change).
  14. FE4's skills: who has them (units, classes, the items that give them) and, for Wrath, Charm, Miracle, Renewal, Astra, Luna and Sol, their numbers (Astra, Luna and Sol can also work with any weapon).
  15. + New skill adds one: a stat bonus, battle bonuses (Atk, Def, Hit, Avoid, Crit, attack speed, for the unit or against the foe, always or only when attacking, attacked, at low HP, at full HP, or as an aura for nearby allies), or an effect from later games (Aether, Ignis, Colossus, Vengeance, Deadeye, Lethality, Sure Strike, Great Shield, Aegis, Pray, Counter, Galeforce, Lifetaker) with its rate and power.
  16. Give a new skill to units and classes, and set its icon, type and help text for the map menu's Skills list. Renaming or deleting a skill updates every unit, class and item that has it.
  17. Maps: each chapter's map drawn with the game's own tiles, with its castles, villages and units on top.
  18. Paint (B): pick a tile in the Tiles panel and paint. Drag across several tiles in the panel to paint them as a block (a house, a castle wall). Right-click (or Alt+click) on the map picks the tile under the pointer. The terrain list in the Tiles panel shows only the tiles of one terrain (all the forest tiles, all the water tiles…).
  19. Fill (G) fills the area of the same tile that you click.
  20. Pick (I): click to pick one tile, or drag over the map to copy a block of tiles and paint it elsewhere.
  21. Select (V): click a castle, village or unit to see it and change its position, AI orders or village gold; drag it to move it. Clicking the ground shows its terrain.
  22. The terrain comes with the tile: painting water makes the cell water for the game too. Show → Terrain colours the map by terrain; the status line under the map names the terrain under the pointer.
  23. Edit tile… (on the Tiles panel, or for the cell you clicked) shows a tile large with how many cells use it.
    • Terrain in this chapter changes what the game thinks the tile is (make a decorative tile a forest, a wall passable…), for every tile.
    • Replace drawing… redraws one of the chapter's own tiles (0–255) from a picture: 16 × 16 pixels, or bigger pixel art (2× to 8×), or any picture (it is shrunk). Colours become the nearest of the chapter's map palettes. Each 8 × 8 quarter that isn't already in the chapter needs a free 8 × 8 tile: the chapter's own (3–22 of them), then a pool of 25 shared by all chapters and handed out from the Prologue on (the dialog says how many are left). Drawings that reuse the map's own grass, water or walls fit best. Redrawing a tile the map doesn't use frees its quarters. Tiles 256–1023 are shared by every chapter and can't be redrawn.
  24. Units come onto the map in groups (deploy lists) as the chapter goes on; the Units panel shows or hides each group.
  25. Event areas outlines each area event of the chapter (Sigurd reaches …); with the Select tool, click its label to open the event.
  26. Area flags shows bits stored with each map cell above the tile number. The game uses them (what for isn't known yet); painting keeps them as they are.
  27. Zoom with − / + or Ctrl+mouse wheel; drag with the middle mouse button (or Shift+drag) to move around.
  28. Events & dialogue: each chapter's script as a list of events in plain words: Turn 3, Sigurd talks to Ayra, Someone visits village 5, Midir fights Gerrard, Sigurd reaches (12, 38)–(17, 43), … with the first line of what's said. Filter by talks, turns, villages, battles and areas, or search all dialogue in the game.
  29. When: the condition's settings as fields (the turn, who talks to whom, which village, the area's corners, who fights whom). Pick people from a list of names.
  30. Then: the steps that follow. Play scene N and Show a message open the conversation right there; Units arrive: deploy list N has a See them on the map button; items given, stat boosts and army changes have their own fields.
  31. Conversations are speech bubbles with the speakers' portraits, left and right as in the game. Each bubble is one box of text (one button press): type into it, Enter starts a new line. Click a portrait to change who speaks. Under each box, a gauge measures the widest line with the game's own letter widths: the box holds 224 pixels and 3 lines. Letters the game has: A–Z, a–z, 0–9, space and . , : ? ! ' - " á é ö.
  32. Show codes shows the whole message with its control codes for anything else (new boxes, pauses, portraits coming and going).
  33. Steps and conditions whose meaning isn't known yet say so; their bytes can still be changed under Bytes (same length).
  34. + New event adds a talk, a turn event, a village visit, an area someone reaches, a battle quote or a death quote. Each new event comes with a fresh conversation to write in; new talks get their own scene and an entry in the chapter's Talk menu, so the map menu offers Talk for that pair until the talk has played. New turn events on a turn that already plays a scene add their message to that scene (the game plays one scene per check).
  35. Add steps to an event (someone says something, give an item, raise a stat, units arrive), remove steps with ×, or Delete event. Messages only a deleted event used go with it. Every one of these is one undo step.
  36. Area events show a picture of their part of the map. Click it (or Pick on map) and drag a box across the chapter map, with castles, villages, starting units and the chapter's other areas drawn on it. New area events have the same picker.
  37. Talk events show whether the pair is in the Talk menu, with a button to add or remove it. Changing who talks keeps the menu entry in step.
  38. Items, shops & villages, in three tabs:
  39. Weapons & items: every weapon, staff and ring, grouped by type. Rename it, and set Might, Hit, Weight, range, uses, price, rank, weapon type, what it's effective against, its special effect (brave, magic sword spell, status edge…), which holy blood it needs, and the skills it gives while equipped. Copies in the game lists each of the item's numbered copies (FE4 has 141) with who starts with it, which shop sells it and which event gives it; any copy can be turned into another item, and unused copies can be turned into this one.
  40. Shops & villages, per chapter: each castle's town buttons (Armory, Pawnbroker, Blacksmith, Supply, Augury, Arena) and what its Armory sells (add or remove item copies; it warns when someone already starts with a copy, since that copy won't go on sale). Each village's gold and what visiting gives (the item, or the stat and amount), with a link to the visit event.
  41. Arena: the seven opponents per chapter for melee and ranged fighters, with each opponent's class and level.
  42. Armies & AI, per chapter: the armies the chapter sets up, by slot (slot 0 is yours).
  43. The army: its name on the map, the castles it holds and its members (units that join when it's set up).
  44. What its units do: each AI program in words (Seize Jungby, From turn 3 on, go to step 4, Attack anyone…), with fields for the castle, turn or target. Change what it does… rewrites a program: attack anyone, attack only what's in reach, seize a castle, guard a castle, raid villages, go after one unit, or stay put, optionally only from a given turn (until then they wait or attack what's in reach). It can also set a marching formation and facing, a player unit they never attack, and an HP % at which they go home to heal. Every command in the game now has a plain description; formation, facing, spared unit and percentages have their own fields.
  45. Units on the map: every deployed unit of the army, grouped by deploy list, with the program it follows. Set all to… changes a whole list at once.
  46. Portraits: all 247 faces with who uses each one (units, generic classes, people who only speak). Pick one to see it large, with a talking preview.
  47. Replace with a picture… takes any image. It is fitted to 48 × 64 (fill and crop, or fit inside) and reduced to the 15 colours a portrait can have. The colour in the corners becomes transparent unless you keep the background.
  48. Download PNG gives the 80 × 64 sheet: the face, plus the two talking-mouth frames at the right (each is the face's 32 × 16 block at the left, 32 pixels down, with the mouth open). Edit it and replace with it to draw your own talking frames.
  49. Use the original goes back to the game's picture. On a character's sheet, Change picture opens that character's portrait.
  50. Map sprites: the little unit each class shows on the map.
  51. The list shows every class's sprite, animated, in the army colour you pick (player, enemy, ally, other, has moved).
  52. Some classes have extra entries the game checks first: one for women, or one for a single character (Knight Lord: Sigurd and Seliph each have their own). Use another sprite… picks from all 63 sprites on foot and 31 mounted or flying ones.
  53. Add a sprite for adds a row for one character (say, Noish gets his own look as a Cavalier) or for all women or all men of the class; Remove takes a row out. Rows are checked from the top, so new ones go above "Everyone else".
  54. Edit drawing shows a sprite's three frames. Replace drawing… takes a PNG of the three frames side by side (48 × 16 on foot, 48 × 24 mounted; 2× to 4× bigger also works); colours become the nearest of the map palette, so every army recolours it. Download PNG gives the current drawing to edit. Classes that share a sprite all change.
  55. Edit walking shows what the unit looks like while it moves (and while you have it picked up): four steps for each direction, with a walker per direction playing them. Replace walking pictures… takes one PNG with the steps across and left, right, up and down from the top (96 × 96 on foot, four 24 × 24 frames per row; 128 × 128 mounted or flying, 32 × 32 frames; 2× to 4× bigger also works), in the same colours as the drawing. Download PNG gives the current pictures to start from; Use the original goes back.
  56. Changes are checked as you type (a value out of range turns red and says why). Undo / Redo (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) cover every change (a whole paint stroke or fill is one step); Save (Ctrl+S) writes the project files.
  57. Build ROM writes the hacked ROM plus a shareable .ips patch next to the project folder. Test play (F6) builds it and opens it straight away in the FE4 PC port (FE4Port.zip), without your mods and with its own saves. The editor finds FE4.exe when the kit sits in the port's fe4kit folder (as it comes in FE4Port.zip) or next to it; otherwise it asks once. Create IPS patch is the one to give other people: name the patch, add who made it and a description, pick where to save it. It writes name.ips plus name.txt, a read-me with your description, which ROM the patch needs (Project Naga, headerless or not, with its SHA-1 and CRC32) and how to apply it (Flips, Lunar IPS, or soft-patching in an emulator). It checks the patch by applying it to your ROM before it finishes. The patch holds only your changes, never the game. The dialog remembers what you typed for next time.
  58. Battle animations lists every class with a row for each weapon kind (and gender, and the few character-only rows such as Julia's). Pick one to watch it: a player runs any of the animation's motions (the game's own scripts: frames, timings and loops) at ¼× to 2× speed, frame by frame or mirrored as the left-hand fighter, with any weapon in hand, plus a strip of every frame and a PNG download. Below it are the four numbers the game uses: Animation (moves, frames and timing), Body graphics (the drawings), Upper body (rider, torso and head tiles) and Palette, each saying which other classes share it. Use its look gives the row another class's look in one step (Undo puts it back). Redraw frames: Download frames to draw on gives a PNG of every frame's body and rider side by side, with the look's two palettes as swatches along the top (the weapon and shadow aren't in it and stay as they are). Paint over it with the swatch colours, keep its size, and Import drawn frames…. Only frames you changed are re-cut into the game's sprite pieces (up to 64 tiles of 8x8 a frame); the import says which frames changed. Your drawings become a new animation and body graphics for that row only (or every row that looks like this), so classes that shared the old ones don't change. Rows using your drawings show ✎; Use the game's animation goes back. Edit motions: each motion is the list of steps the game plays: frames with how long each shows (in 1/60 s) and commands (Wait, Go back, Start over, Signal / Wait for signal between the two fighters, effects, End). Change frames and times, reorder, remove or add steps; the player shows the change before you Save motion. New frame: a copy of frame N adds a frame you can then redraw and use in any motion (up to 230 frames). Like redrawing, this makes a new animation for that row (or every row that looks like this). The standing pose is motion 0, which the game shows but doesn't time, so a standing loop won't move in battle. Spells (the switch above the class list) is the list of items that play a spell effect in battle: tomes, staves and the magic swords. Pick which effect each one plays (the effects are named after the item that uses them in the game), and the one it plays "at range" (Physic and the status staves cast from far away). Give an item an effect adds any item; it only shows if that attacker's animation casts (a plain sword swing or thrown axe ignores it). Saved in spells.csv. Spell pictures (the third switch) lists every effect that draws pictures: fireballs, flame pillars, lightning, Naga's dragon, the staff sparkles. Pick one to see how its pictures are laid out on screen and each picture as a sheet of 8x8 tiles. Download a sheet, paint over it and Import… it back at the same size; Use the game's goes back. The colours in the sheets are stand-ins: each is a colour number, and the battle supplies the real colours (they cycle and fade while the spell plays), so paint with the colours already in the sheet (anything else is matched to the nearest). Background sheets have 15 colours plus transparent, overlay sheets 3. A picture several spells share (Fire and Elfire's fireball) says so, and changes for all of them. Saved as spell_gfx/rNNN.png; the build packs each into free space. Battle colours / Stand-in colours switches the pictures between the colours the spell sets and the stand-ins. Under Colours are the spell's own colour sets (set at the start, cycling, changing every few frames, or fading): click a colour to change it, or use Hue, Saturation and Brightness with Apply to this spell to recolour the whole effect (blue fire, green thunder). Colour numbers the spell doesn't set take whatever the battle scene has there. Saved in spell_gfx/colors.json. New effect from this one… makes your own effect: a copy with its own pictures and colours, so you can make Blue Fire and keep Fire. It starts with any changes you made to the original, shows in the list as NEW, and is picked for items on the Spells tab like any other effect. Rename… and Delete (once no item uses it) are on its page. Saved in spell_gfx/effects.json (plus sNN_rNNN.png pictures and sNN: colours); the build moves the game's spell tables to free space to make room. Steps lists what the effect does, in order: load pictures, colour effects, background motions, sounds, pauses, waits ("Wait for the steps above to finish") and the signals that tell the fighters' animations to go on. Change a Pause length (60 frames = 1 second) or a Sound number, move, copy or remove steps, Add a pause or Add a sound, then Save steps (Use the game's steps goes back). Each cycling or changing colour set also has Changes every N frames to speed its colours up or slow them down. Saved in spell_gfx/steps.json and spell_gfx/timing.json. Removing the Signal or Hit steps can leave a fighter waiting forever, so check changes with Watch in battle. Background steps move the effect's pictures: Move slides them a number of pixels across and down each frame for a number of frames (the game mirrors it for the other side), Fly to the target sends them at the target at a speed (higher is faster: Fire's fireball is 12). Other effects can share a background step; make a new effect to keep them apart. Saved in spell_gfx/motions.json. Watch in battle shows the effect in a real battle in the PC port (0.9 or newer, set in Settings): it builds a copy of your ROM with your battle and spell changes only, races through the Prologue to the fight where Gandolf attacks Midir, makes Gandolf a Fire Mage casting your effect, and replays that battle until you close the window. The first time takes about a minute; then it starts straight at the battle. Skill animations (the fourth switch) is the sparkle each skill shows when it activates: Astra, Luna, Sol and Pavise; Adept, Vantage and Wrath, which show nothing in the game but can have a sparkle now (Adept's plays before the extra strike, Vantage's when it makes the defender strike first, Wrath's on each strike it makes critical); plus every new skill you made on the Skills page from later games (Aether, Lethality, Great Shield and the rest). For each one pick the Animation (the game's sparkles, or your own), its five Sparkle colours (click one, or use Hue, Saturation and Brightness and Apply colours), and for defence skills what happens When the hit lands (Pavise's shield, or nothing). A new skill starts with the sparkle of the skill it borrows from (most attack skills borrow Luna's) or none; it can have nothing at all. Make it its own sparkle gives the skill a copy of the animation (a new effect), so Edit its pictures and steps (Spell pictures) changes only it. Use the game's goes back. Watch in battle plays the sparkle in the PC port at the start of the same Prologue battle as for spells, so you can see it whatever the skill really needs to activate. Saved in skill_anims.csv.

Map battles (the fifth switch) is what fights on the map when battle animations are off: two little figures. Each class shows a figure (most their own; some classes have another for women, and Sigurd with Tyrfing has his own). Pick any figure for a class under Figure and Women show. A figure has a sheet for each way it can face its foe (left, up-left, up, right, down-right, down; up-right uses the right sheet and down-left the left one): 256 tiles as the game keeps them, 16 across, the poses in 16 × 16 pieces with weapons and mounts further down. Download one, paint over the pieces and Replace… it (128 × 128, or 2× to 4× that, in the map palette's colours); every class showing the figure changes. Watch a map battle opens the PC port on the Prologue fight as a map battle, with Midir shown as the class's figure. How it moves plays the figure's motions (standing and its attacks) for the way it faces and the weapon it holds, drawn from its sheets, so a redrawn sheet shows at once. Saved in map_battle.csv and mapbattle/.

Map spells (the sixth switch) is what tomes and staves show on the map in those battles: the fireball, bolt, rock or sparkle that flies to the target (Fire, Thunder, Wind, Light, Meteor and the rest, plus Return, Warp, Rescue, Restore, Sleep and Thief). Each spell has a sheet of 32 tiles (128 × 16); Download, paint and Replace… it in the spell's colours. Colours are one of six sets the spells share: pick which one a spell uses, change a colour by clicking it, or shift them all with the sliders (every spell using that set changes). Watch on the map opens the PC port on the Prologue fight with Midir casting the tome. Every spell (also Forseti, Blizzard, Aura and the healing staves, which have no flying piece) has a Background too: the big picture drawn over the darkened map while it plays (Meteor's falling rock, Fire's flame trail, Forseti's storm). Download it, paint over it and Replace… it (256 × 256, up to 128 different 8 × 8 tiles, mirrored ones count once); each 8 × 8 square keeps the colours the game gives it there. The colours shown are close, not exact, because the game cycles each spell's colours itself while it plays. Saved in map_spells.csv, map_palettes.csv and mapfx/. 15. On the Project page, Add unit stats to the map box puts the PC port's MT / HIT / AS / AVO box into the ROMs and patches you build, so players on emulators get it too (the port can still switch it off).

The classic editor (link on the Project page, or run BuilderOfTheHolyWar.exe classic) still has every table as rows, for the few things the new editor doesn't show yet (AI orders, armies, love growth). Adding or removing event steps is done by hand in the .evt files (see below). In the classic editor, pick a page on the left, select a row, edit it in the panel on the right and press Apply. Its Build menu also has Test generation 2, which writes a copy whose New Game opens on chapter 6, and Test play generation 2 in the PC port.

Maps in projects from kit v15 or older: those kits read the chapter 5 and endgame maps wrongly (and drew the chapter 9 map picture with the wrong tiles). The editor re-reads those two maps from your ROM when it opens the project, as long as they haven't been edited; building still works with the old files either way.

The project folder is plain CSV and text files, so you can also edit it in a spreadsheet. The editor, the classic editor and the Python scripts below all read the same projects. Run the editor from Python with python fe4studio.py.

Randomizer

The Randomizer page (or BuilderOfTheHolyWar.exe rando ROM out.smc --preset standard) makes a randomized ROM, an .ips patch and a spoiler log, from the clean game or from your own hack.

Option What it does
Growth rates vary each growth, redistribute the same total, or pick freely between a min and max
Personal base stats vary or redistribute each character's stat bonuses
Playable classes a random class of the same tier and gender; starting weapons change to match
Keep Sigurd, Seliph and Julia / dancers story units and dancers keep their class
Healers stay healers units who start with staves get a class that can use them
Holy blood keeps its weapon major blood (Lewyn's Forseti, Quan's Gáe Bolg…) and carried holy weapons stay usable. Children follow the parent they're tied to
Personal skills random personal skills, plus extra ones
Skills from later games Aether, Galeforce, Vengeance, Great Shield, Lifetaker, Pray, Counter… join the pool
Random minor holy blood generation-1 units may gain a minor blood, which their children inherit
Child parents split gives each sibling a different mother. any_parent ties children to fathers too, e.g. Ced to Lewyn and Fee to Ayra
Children per parent at most this many children per parent
Inheritance weights how much of each parent the children get
Love affinities random starting love and growth for every pair
Weapon stats Mt, Hit and Wt of ordinary weapons vary
Enemy classes generic enemies get a class that moves on the same terrain; their weapons follow
  • Presets: light, standard and chaos.
  • Seeds: the same seed and options always make the same ROM.
  • Substitutes: a substitute always shares a weapon type with the child it replaces, because they share their starting items.

Command line (Python 3, no extra packages)

python fe4hack.py extract  "Fire Emblem....smc"  myhack          # once: dump editable CSVs
python fe4hack.py build    "Fire Emblem....smc"  myhack  myhack.smc  --ips myhack.ips
python fe4hack.py build    "Fire Emblem....smc"  myhack  myhack.smc  --hover-stats   # with the unit stats box
python fe4hack.py diff     "Fire Emblem....smc"  myhack.smc     # see exactly what changed
python fe4rando.py "Fire Emblem....smc" rando.smc --seed 42 --preset chaos --opt parents=any_parent
python fe4rando.py --list                                        # every randomizer option
python fe4rando.py --list-tsv options.tsv                        # the same, for other programs
python fe4rando.py ... --log run.txt                             # output and errors to a file

BuilderOfTheHolyWar.exe extract|build|diff|rando … takes the same arguments.

Play the ROM in the FE4 PC port or any SNES emulator: Snes9x, bsnes or RetroArch. The .ips file is a shareable patch, since it contains only your changes; PC port players drop it in their mods folder. The PC port also runs this randomizer from its own menu (Mods → Randomizer).

Column conventions

  • index is the unit ID in the unit files and list.entry in deploy.csv.
  • Rows can be edited in every file. Rows can also be added to units_generic.csv, units_children.csv and deploy.csv, and removed from deploy.csv (see "Adding new things").
  • portrait (all four unit files) is the face a unit shows. Write a number or another unit's name to borrow theirs, e.g. Lex.
  • Unit stats (str … res) are bonuses added to the class bases, which is how FE4 itself works. hp and lck are absolute.
  • Item columns
  • Playable and children files use item instance numbers (see item_instances.csv).
  • NPC and generic files use item type numbers (see items.csv).
  • 255 = empty.
  • Skills are written by name, separated by ;: Pursuit;Astra;Sol. Names follow skills.csv. Use bit3 and so on for unnamed bits.
  • Holy blood is written like Baldur:major;Naga:minor.
  • Weapon ranks are A, B, C, -, or * for holy weapons.
  • Columns starting with _ are for reference only and ignored on build.
  • Names: the _name columns are reference only. Rename things in names.csv.

Events and dialogue

.evt lines look like this:

86C8A7: 02 00 00 00 01 00      ; COND_TURN      turn 1
908106: 30 FF 18 00            ; RUN_SCENE      scene 24
  • Edit the hex bytes; the part after ; is a comment.
  • An edited command must keep its length. To add or remove commands, see "Adding new things".
  • Examples of edits: change which scene a talk or visit triggers, the turn a reinforcement wave arrives, a deploy list number, or a unit ID in a condition.
  • Names ending in ? are educated guesses.
  • OP_xx / SC_xx commands aren't identified yet, but they still edit safely.

dialogue.txt holds one block per message:

@602487  ; ch00 908141 TALK
{07}{00 3D}{00 3F 07 00}{09}Urgh...{02}
Milady, I...{02}
Forgive me...{08}{01}
  • Change the words freely, and make a message as long as you like.
  • Changed messages are moved into empty ROM space.
  • Every script that uses the message is repointed.
  • Keep the {..} control codes:
  • {02} = new line
  • {08} = wait for button
  • {06}/{07} = left/right speaker
  • {00 3F pp 00} = portrait
  • {01} = end
  • Line breaks in the file itself are ignored.
  • Lines in the game box fit about 44 characters.

talks.csv is the map menu's Talk list for each chapter:

column meaning
chapter 0–11
flag the event flag the talk event sets; once set, Talk is no longer offered
from unit who talks, or any
to unit talked to
needs empty = always; gold10000 = only while the talker has 10,000 gold (hiring Beowulf)
flag_chapter the chapter whose flags hold flag (normally the same chapter)

A row only makes Talk appear. What happens is the chapter's talk event (COND_TALK 04, or 16 from chapter 6 on) with the same units and flag. Same-army talks need both; the editor's + New event writes both.

Maps

  • Easiest: the Maps page of Builder of the Holy War (above).
  • Without the editor: open map_editor.html in a browser. Load a chapter's maps/chNN_*.csv and its chNN_*_tiles.png, then paint:
  • Click a tile, or drag across the tile sheet to pick a block such as a castle.
  • Right-click the map to copy tiles from it.
  • Fill box paints a rectangle.
  • Save the CSV back into maps/.
  • By hand: edit the CSV values directly. Each number is a tile from the tile sheet, counted left to right, top to bottom, 32 per row.
  • Rules:
  • Keep the same number of cells (every map is 64 × 64).
  • The bits above the tile number (value >> 10) are per-cell area flags; keep them.
  • Terrain follows the tile, so water tiles block walking.
  • Edited maps are recompressed and stored in free ROM space automatically.
  • Previews: PNGs need Pillow (pip install pillow). Everything else needs only Python.

Castles and villages

locations.csv has one row per castle or village in each chapter.

Column Applies to What it does
x, y both A castle's seize point, or the spot you stand on to visit a village
castle_id castles The castle's name, from the game's list of 46 castles
facilities castles Castle-town buttons, separated by ;: Armory, Pawnbroker, Blacksmith, Supply, Augury, Arena. Use - for none
shop castles What the Armory sells: item instance numbers separated by ; (see item_instances.csv). Usually only the chapter's first castle has one
gold villages Gold paid on the first visit, 1–50000 (the game's default is 5000 for every village)
  • The map is separate: the castle or village graphics are map tiles, so paint them in the map painter too when you move one.
  • Previews: map PNGs outline each location, labelled with its index.
  • Shop rules:
  • Each item instance is a single physical item, so a shop sells one of each.
  • An item only goes on sale if nobody owns it yet when the chapter starts.
  • The Armory button must be in facilities for the shop to be reachable. The Prologue has neither by default, but both can be added.
  • Shops can be longer than the original; extra space comes from free space in the same ROM bank (about 300–2,500 bytes per chapter group).
  • Village gold only changes the first-visit payout. Brigands still loot it as normal.
  • Limits: rows can't be added yet, and a castle can't become a village.

What a village gives besides gold

Item and stat rewards are scene commands in events/scenes.evt. Find the scene from the village's COND_VISIT line in the chapter's .evt file:

B18627: 4A 57 FF FF 7B   ; EVENT  GIVE_ITEM visitor gets Speed ring(#123)
9D914C: 4A 70 FF 05 01   ; EVENT  STAT_UP visitor Def +1
  • GIVE_ITEM: the last byte is the item instance. FF FF means the visiting unit.
  • STAT_UP: the bytes are the unit (FF = visitor), the stat (0 HP, 1 Str, 2 Mag, 3 Skl, 4 Spd, 5 Def, 6 Res, 7 Lck) and the amount.

Arena

arena.csv lists the 7 arena opponents for each chapter.

  • melee / ranged are unit IDs. The game uses the ranged opponent when your fighter's weapon reaches 2 or more spaces.
  • Changing an opponent's stats: opponents are ordinary units, so their class, level and weapons are in units_generic.csv (IDs 480 onward).
  • Rank 8 rows are fallbacks. If the rank 7 opponent has already joined your army, the game uses rank 8 instead. That is how chapter 2's Holyn is replaced by Chaimos once he joins.
  • New arenas: the Prologue and chapter 6 have no arena. Add 7 rows for that chapter, and add Arena to its home castle's facilities.

Armies and enemy AI

FE4 groups units into armies, for example "Evans" (Gandolf's Verdane force) or "Jungby". Three files work together:

  • Which army a unit is in: in deploy.csv, army_slot puts the unit in one of the chapter's army slots (0 is always you). The chapter's opening script fills the slots with SET_ARMY lines, such as 4B 01 02 ; slot 1 = army 2.
  • armies.csv: one row per army.
  • name: the castle name it uses.
  • members: units that join it when it's set up. Sigurd's army lists Sigurd, Noish, Alec and Arden.
  • castles: location indices it holds from the start. When two armies claim the same castle, the one set up later wins.
  • _orders: how many AI programs it has.
  • How a unit behaves: orders in deploy.csv picks one of its army's programs (1, 2, …; 0 = stands still). The programs are in ai_orders.evt:
== army 2 orders 2 @9081A8
9081A8: 1F 03 AE 81   ; IF_TURN_GOTO      turn >= 3 -> 81AE
9081AC: 0A            ; ATTACK_IN_REACH
9081AD: FF            ; END
9081AE: 06 01 01      ; SEIZE_CASTLE      location 1 (Jungby)
9081B1: 06 00 01      ; SEIZE_CASTLE      location 0 (Chalphy)
9081B4: 18            ; ATTACK_ANY

Each turn a unit runs its program from the top, and the first command that can act does. This one means "until turn 3, only attack what's in reach; from turn 3, march on Jungby, then Chalphy, then attack anyone". Change 03 to 01 and that group marches on turn 1. - Useful commands:

Command Bytes Meaning
SEIZE_CASTLE 06 loc mode march on and take an enemy castle
GUARD_CASTLE 07 loc mode go back to and hold one of its own castles
RAID_VILLAGE 08 loc destroy a village
RAID_ANY_VILLAGE 17 destroy any village
ATTACK_IN_REACH 0A attack only what it can reach this turn
ATTACK_ANY 18 go after any enemy
ATTACK_UNIT 0D unit target one unit (word)
MOVE_TO 09 flag bit x y walk to (x, y) unless the event flag is set; set it on arrival
IF_TURN_GOTO 1F turn addr jump once the turn number is reached
IF_FLAG_GOTO / IF_NOT_FLAG_GOTO 20/21 flag bit addr jump on an event flag
GOTO 01 addr jump
STOP / END 00 / FF do nothing more this turn
  • Settings and retreats (v30; decoded from the game's code, see ROM_MAP.md). Settings change how later commands behave and then go on to the next line, so they go at the top of a program:
Command Bytes Meaning
FORMATION 0C shape facing (not yet seen working in a test) march as a group in a shape (0 none, 1 narrow wedge, 2 wide wings, 3 arrowhead, 4 triangle, 5 one row, 6 two rows, 7 single file, 8–9 columns two/three wide, 10 wide V, 11 zigzag, 12 ring, 13–14 clusters); facing 0 south, 1 west, 2 north, 3 east
PACE 03 n move only about n/256 of its movement each turn (00 = full)
SPARE_SLOT 04 slot never attack the player unit in that army slot (e.g. 04 12 spares Raquesis)
RETREAT_IF_HURT 1E n 00 01 go back to its castle to heal when HP ≤ n/256 (4C ≈ 30%)
RETREAT_IF_GROUP_LOW 11 n go back to its castle when less than n/256 of its group is left
RETREAT / RETREAT_IF_CLEAR 0F / 10 go back to its castle (always / when no enemy is near)
HOLD 13 stay in place
ATTACK_IN_REACH_OR_GO_ON 0B attack what's in reach, else go on to the next line
FOLLOW_UNIT 0E unit n stay within n tiles of a unit (word)
IF_NO_TARGET_GOTO 22 addr jump when nothing can be attacked this turn
REINFORCE_EVERY 14 n every n turns, sends out group members who are off the map (unconfirmed)
  • Editing rules: the same as the other .evt files (see "Adding new things"). Jump addresses are the low 16 bits of an address in the same bank. A new line can jump to another command of the same program by its position instead: @L3 is the program's 4th command (counting from 0), wherever the build puts it:
== army 2 orders 1 @9081A0
+ 1F 03 @L2   ; from turn 3, go to the third command
+ 00          ; until then: stay put
+ 18          ; attack anyone
+ FF
  • Old projects: older deploy.csv files use army and faction for orders and army_slot, and they still build.

Skills

skills.csv has one row per skill. Rows 1–29 are FE4's own skills; add rows from id 30 up for new ones.

Who has a skill

Column Gives the skill to Notes
personal_bit units that list it in their skills column 24 bits; 22 and 23 are free
class_bit classes that list it in classes.csv skills 16 bits; 13–15 are free
item_bit anyone carrying an item/ring that lists it 16 bits; 13–15 are free
units these units, e.g. Sigurd;Ayra;769 new skills only, no bit needed
classes these classes, e.g. Swordmaster;Bow Knight new skills only, no bit needed

Bits are scarce, so for new skills use units and classes. They take names or index numbers. Write the index when a name repeats: Finn has two records, so he is 5 or 34.

FE4's own skills

stat, amount and amount2 tune the built-in effects:

Skill What the numbers mean Game default
Astra, Luna, Sol activation % = stat × amount / 100 + amount2 Skl × 100 + 0
Wrath crit % when HP ≤ half 100
Charm hit/avoid bonus given to nearby allies 10
Miracle below amount HP, avoid + (amount − HP) × amount2 11, 10
Renewal heals amount + random(amount2) HP per turn 5, 6
  • weapons: in FE4, Astra, Luna and Sol only trigger with swords. Set weapons to any and they trigger with any weapon except staves. Leave it blank, or set sword, to keep FE4's rule.
  • stat: Str, Mag, Skl, Spd, Def, Res or Lck.

New skills (id 30+)

The effect column picks what a new skill does.

stat: a permanent stat bonus, stat + amount (1–30), e.g. Str 5. It shows on the status page like a ring.

combat: battle bonuses, written in bonus with an optional when.

Column Values
bonus atk, def, hit, avoid, crit, speed with + or −, separated by ;. Prefix foe_ to change the enemy's number instead.
when blank = always, attacking (you started the fight), defending, hp<=50, hp<=25, hp_full, ally_near

ally_near turns the skill into an aura: allies within 3 spaces of the skill holder get the bonus, but the holder doesn't. The bonuses show in the battle forecast. Examples:

Skill bonus when
Death Blow atk+6 attacking
Darting Blow speed+5 attacking
Defiant Str atk+7 hp<=25
Fortune foe_crit-100
Hit +20 hit+20
Rally atk+3;def+3 ally_near

Skills from later games. The activation rate is stat × amount / 100 + amount2, like Astra; blank columns use the defaults below. stat can also be Lv or none. power is the effect's strength.

effect What it does Default rate power
aether ignores defence and heals the damage dealt (Sol + Luna) Skl × 50%
ignis damage + a stat Skl Def 50 (half Def)
colossus damage + a stat Str Str 100
vengeance damage + HP lost Skl × 200% 50 (half)
deadeye damage × power % Skl × 50% 200
lethality defeats the enemy outright Skl × 25%
sure_strike attacks always hit Skl
great_shield blocks a hit completely (Pavise effect) Lv
aegis damage × power % from magic or ranged hits Skl 50
pray survive a deadly hit with 1 HP (needs 2+ HP) Lck
counter the attacker takes the same damage when hitting from 1 space (never below 1 HP) always 100
galeforce player units: after defeating an enemy, act again always
lifetaker after defeating an enemy, heal power % of max HP always 50
  • When they trigger: attack skills work like Astra/Luna/Sol. They are rolled when the unit's attack starts, apply to that whole attack, always hit, and show Luna's or Sol's battle effect. Defensive skills roll on each hit.
  • Nihil: blocks both attack and defensive skills from the other side.
  • Weapons: new attack skills work with any weapon except staves. Set weapons to sword for FE4's swords-only rule.
  • Counter: its damage shows on the map after the battle, not in the battle animation.

The Skills menu

New skills appear in the map menu's Skills list, after FE4's 18. Each one gets its name, an icon, a type and a help text, and the bearers are listed just like FE4's own skills.

Column Meaning
menu no hides the skill
icon borrow a listed skill's icon (Pavise, Luna, Charm…), or use one of the six unused ones, spare1–spare6
type Combat, Field or Sword
description help text, wrapped to 3 lines; / forces a line break. Defaults to a short description of the effect.

Limits: - The menu holds 32 rows, so up to 14 new skills can be listed. - The names share 57 spaces 8 pixels wide. A name like "Vengeance" uses 7 and "Rally" uses 3, and a name can be at most 56 pixels. - Help text is limited to 3 lines of about 96 pixels each. The build tells you when something doesn't fit.

Other limits

  • New skills don't show yet in the row of skill icons on a unit's status page.
  • Changing the names of FE4's own skills in the menu isn't supported yet.
  • The effects of Pavise, Adept, Vantage, Accost, Pursuit and Paragon are fixed. Who has them can still be changed.

Generation 2

Everything about who the children are and what they get from their parents is in units_children.csv, lovers.csv and gen2_rules.csv.

How children are born

At the end of chapter 5 FE4 makes each married mother's two children, then sends the generation-1 cast away. The kit replaces that step:

  • Any unit can be a child's parent, father or mother. The other parent is whoever that unit married. FE4's pairs are the defaults: Ulster and Larcei are Ayra's, Ced and Fee are Erin's, and so on.
  • To tie Ced to Lewyn instead of Erin, write Lewyn in Ced's parent. Ced is then Lewyn's son with whoever Lewyn marries. Fee stays Erin's daughter.
  • A parent can have any number of children: give several rows the same parent.
  • Children are made in row order, one couple at a time.

units_children.csv columns

Column Meaning
parent the unit the child is tied to (name or ID)
always yes: born even if that parent has died. FE4 does this for Deirdre and Ethlyn. The parent still has to have married
born_flag event flag set when the child is born. Scripts test it to choose the child or the substitute. Fixed for FE4's own children; new children get one from 128 up when left blank
substitute who appears instead when the child isn't born (Rodolbahn for Ulster, …)
joins for new children: the chapter (6–11) whose opening adds them to Seliph's army
inherit_skills which of the parents' personal skills pass down, e.g. Pursuit;Astra. Blank on FE4's own children means none; blank on a new child means all
growth_father, growth_mother percent of each parent's growth rates the child gets. FE4: 100 from the same-gender parent, 50 from the other
stats_father, stats_mother percent of each parent's stats added to the child's base. FE4: 20 and 10
heir father or mother: whose holy blood passes in full and whose items the child inherits

Example (Ced as Lewyn's son, with more of his father's growths):

index,_name,...,parent,always,born_flag,substitute,joins,inherit_skills,growth_father,growth_mother,...
53,Ced,...,Lewyn,,25,Hawke,,Pursuit;Adept;...,120,50,...

New children

Add a row to units_children.csv with the next free ID (769, or after your new generic units). Fill in at least class, level, gender, parent and joins. Blank cells get defaults.

  • name_id defaults to an empty name slot (570–575). Name it in names.csv, e.g. character,570,Kian. The build stops if a new unit has no name.
  • portrait: borrow one, e.g. Lex.
  • slot (the army and love slot) defaults to the substitute's slot, or to 128 (none) if there is no substitute.
  • Joining: joins adds the child at the start of that chapter (or the substitute, if the child wasn't born). The commands go into the chapter's opening scene. To recruit them some other way, write your own events and test the child's born_flag.

Limits of new children:

  • Saves. FE4's save slot is nearly full at the start of chapter 6: about 1,900 of its 1,982 bytes with the usual 17 children. Every extra child who is born adds about 50 bytes, so one extra child fits. More than that can break saving. The build prints a note when more than 18 children can be born. Substitutes cost nothing extra.
  • Love. New children have no love slot, so they can't fall in love or have lover conversations.
  • Unit lists. Lists such as the Unit list draw names from a pre-drawn sheet, and generation 2's sheet has room for about 5 more tiles (one short name).
  • Any further names show blank in those lists only. Status pages, battles and dialogue show them normally.
  • Renamed characters are redrawn in their sheet when they fit.
  • Memory. FE4 keeps children in a heap that holds exactly 17. Extra children go in the generation-1 heap, which the departing cast frees.

Substitutes

Changing substitute puts the new unit wherever a script picks between the child and the substitute, such as the chapter-6 opening.

  • Conversations written for the old substitute still use the old one. Edit those in events/ if needed.
  • A generation-1 character used as a substitute shows a wrong name in unit lists, because the lists use generation 2's name sheet.

Testing generation 2 quickly

dev/ch6start.py makes a test copy of your built ROM whose New Game opens straight on chapter 6, with the generation-1 cast married as you choose:

python dev/ch6start.py myhack.smc test6.smc --marry Lewyn-Ayra,Lex-Erin --single Bridget

The children are born through the real transition, with your parent links and rules. Generation-1 stats are their starting values, so the children come out weaker than in a full playthrough.

Names

names.csv has one row per name the game draws. Change the name column to rename it.

kind id is
character the name_id from the unit files (Sigurd = 0)
class the class number (classes.csv index)
item the item number (items.csv / rings.csv index)
castle the castle number (castle_id in locations.csv)
  • How it works: Project Naga stores English names as pre-drawn pictures, so the build draws the new name with the game's own name font. That font was lifted from the existing names and re-draws them pixel-for-pixel. The new picture goes into free space.
  • Castles are drawn in all five places they appear, including the map's terrain window.
  • The class and item text tables are updated too.
  • Characters you can use: letters, digits, spaces and . , ' - ! ? : / & ( ).
  • Length limits: the build stops if a name is too wide for its window. Roughly 7–9 letters, depending on the letters.
Kind Widest
Characters 7 tiles (56 px)
Classes 8 tiles
Items 9 tiles
Castles 7 tiles
  • Several slots per name: generic units share names such as "Verdane", and each slot is renamed separately. To rename one enemy group, give its units a name_id of their own.
  • Empty slots: character slots 570–575 are empty and listed too. Use them to name new units.
  • Unit lists: the Unit list and similar screens use a separate pre-drawn sheet per generation. Renamed player characters are redrawn there too, when the sheet has room (see "Generation 2").
  • Not covered: names inside dialogue are ordinary text in events/dialogue.txt.

Adding new things

New enemies in a chapter. Add rows to deploy.csv with the next entry number in a list, e.g. 0.34 and 0.35 after the Prologue's 0.33.

  • Fill in every column. army_slot 1 is the Prologue's Verdane army, and orders 0 makes a unit stand still.
  • To remove an entry, delete its row.
  • Each list is spawned by an event DEPLOY command, so its units appear when that command runs. List 0 is the Prologue's opening army.
  • Lists that grow move into free space automatically (about 4.6 KB is free). New list numbers can't be added.

New unit types. Add rows to units_generic.csv with IDs from 769 up: 769, 770, … (new children in units_children.csv share the same numbering).

  • IDs 570–768 aren't units: the game's scripts use them for the portraits of people who only talk (narrators, villagers). The kit keeps them that way.
  • Projects from before v12 that start at 570 still build, with a note.
  • Saves now keep unit IDs in 10 bits instead of 9, so units past 511 survive saving and loading. That makes saves from an earlier build of your hack unreadable.
  • Copy char_id and name_id from the unit you based it on, e.g. 69 and 68 for a Verdane axe fighter. Menus take the name from name_id, and the battle screen takes it from name slot char_id − 1.
  • Then use the new ID in deploy.csv, arena.csv or armies.csv.
  • About 30 new units fit.

New script commands. In any .evt file (events/chNN_*.evt, events/scenes.evt, ai_orders.evt), a line starting with + adds a command after the line above it, and deleting a line removes a command:

908040: 30 FF 01 00   ; RUN_SCENE scene 1
+ 57 01 00 7B         ; new: Sigurd starts with the Speed ring
908044: FF FF         ; END
  • A block that changes size is moved into free space and everything that starts it is repointed. That means the chapter header, the scene table or the army's orders list.
  • Jumps inside the block are fixed up. Write jump targets as the original addresses shown in the file.
  • The build checks each new command against the game's own length rules and names the line if one is wrong.
  • Some AI programs share code with one listed earlier, marked "(shared, listed above)". Add commands to the earlier program instead.

New messages. In events/dialogue.txt, a block headed @N00001 (@N and five digits) is a new message. Use it in any .evt file by writing @N00001 where its 3-byte address goes:

+ 0C @N00001          ; SC_TEXT  a new line of dialogue
  • The build puts new messages into free space and fills in their addresses.
  • The editor makes these for you when you add an event or a step.

New conversations (scenes). 88 of the 562 scenes are empty and nothing starts them. The editor uses those for new events: it writes the scene in scenes.evt under its scene N label and starts it with RUN_SCENE. Only one scene plays per event check, so an event should start one scene.
